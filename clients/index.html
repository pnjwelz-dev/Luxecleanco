Tips & Tricks

Real cleaning tips from Peg, the way she actually does it at home and on the job. Pick one, try it, and make your whole week a little easier.

CLEANING TIP #1

Baked-on mess? Don't scrub it to death! Before you spend forever on baked-on food and grease, let the soak do the work for you. The soak helps soften and loosen stuck-on food and grease, making the cleanup much easier.

  1. Fill your dish or pan with hot water
  2. Add a little dish soap
  3. Toss in 1 dryer sheet
  4. Let it soak a few hours — or overnight for stubborn messes

Luxe tip: Always check the manufacturer’s care instructions first. Avoid abrasive products on nonstick or delicate finishes — and don’t soak anything that shouldn’t be submerged.

CAR INTERIOR TIP #3

Your car hauls kids, groceries, dogs, and half your life — and it shows. You don't need a detail shop to make it feel brand new again.

  1. Pull the mats and trash out, then vacuum seats and cracks
  2. Wipe the dash and cup holders with a damp microfiber cloth
  3. Sprinkle baking soda on seats, wait 15 minutes, then vacuum
  4. Tuck an air freshener under a seat — it lasts longer there

Luxe tip: Skip household cleaners on leather seats and touchscreens — they dry leather out and can fog up your screen. Use products made for the job.

GLASS CLEANING TIP #4

Nothing makes a clean room look dirty faster than streaky glass. Mirrors, windows, glass doors — here's how we get every one of them crystal clear.

  1. Dust the glass first with a dry microfiber — don't smear dirt around
  2. Spray the cleaner on your cloth, not the glass — no drips on sills or frames
  3. Wipe top to bottom in an S-pattern — circles leave swirls
  4. Flip to the dry side and buff — that's what kills the streaks

Luxe Glass Spray: 2 cups water + 1/2 cup white vinegar + 1/4 tsp dish soap. Mix it in a spray bottle, spray, and wipe with a microfiber cloth.

If someone vapes or smokes inside, that oily yellow film sticks to the windows and regular cleaner just smears it around. Cut through it with a damp magic eraser first, then follow with the Luxe Glass Spray. To keep it from building back up, wipe the windows down often and crack a window for fresh air when you can.

Luxe tip: Skip the ammonia cleaner on tinted windows — it'll eat the tint right off over time. Ammonia-free glass cleaner keeps tint safe and still cuts the grime.

VAPE & SMOKER GLASS TIP

Vape clouds and cigarette smoke leave an oily, yellow film on glass that regular cleaner just smears around. If your mirrors and windows never look truly clean — this is why. Here's our fix.

  1. Scrub the glass with a damp magic eraser — it melts through oily film and nicotine buildup
  2. Dry it down with a microfiber towel
  3. Spray our homemade glass cleaner right on the glass
  4. Wipe clean with a microfiber towel
  5. Buff with a brand-new dry microfiber towel — that's your streak-free finish

Luxe Glass Spray: 2 cups water + 1/2 cup white vinegar + 1/4 tsp dish soap. Mix it in a spray bottle — this is the homemade glass cleaner in the steps above.

To keep the film from building back up, wipe the windows down every week or two and crack a window for fresh air when you can. The longer that film sits, the harder it is to cut through.

Luxe tip: Swap your magic eraser often — once it's brown with nicotine, it's just spreading the film back around instead of lifting it off.

LAUNDRY TIP #6

Laundry is the job that never ends, so let's make it end faster. A few small habits will keep your clothes fresher, your colors brighter, and your washer from smelling like a swamp.

  1. Sort into three piles: lights, darks, and delicates — colors stay true
  2. Check every pocket before it goes in the wash
  3. Wash in cold unless it's truly greasy — saves the clothes and the bill
  4. Hang shirts and pull them straight as soon as the dryer stops — wrinkles never get a chance

Luxe tip: Leave the washer door open after a load so it airs out. A shut door traps moisture and breeds mildew — that's the musty smell.

LAUNDRY TIP

Tossing everything in together is how clothes fade, towels go stiff, and sheets come out smelling funky. Sorting is the whole trick — and your clothes will thank you for it.

  1. Whites go together, hot water — keeps them white
  2. Darks stay with darks, cold water — colors don't bleed or fade
  3. Lights get their own load so nothing dulls them
  4. Bedding washes alone — sheets need room to actually get clean

Luxe tip: Wash towels with towels. They're heavy and linty — they'll rough up your clothes and steal the softness from everything else.

COLD WEATHER HOME PREP

Professional tips from Tyler at Cole's Appliance Repair. Before temperatures really drop, check these things to keep your heating system running safely and efficiently.

  1. Change your furnace filter — a dirty filter restricts airflow and makes your system work harder.
  2. Check your vents and registers — make sure they're open and not blocked by furniture, rugs, or curtains.
  3. Keep the area around your furnace clear — don't store boxes or supplies against your heating equipment.
  4. Test your thermostat early — don't wait for the first freezing night to learn your heat isn't working.
  5. Listen for anything unusual — strange noises, odd smells, or weak airflow mean it's time for a checkup.

Luxe tip: A little prevention goes a long way — handle the small maintenance now and stay comfortable all winter. Huge thanks to Tyler at Cole's Appliance Repair for sharing his expertise with our community!

SOCK DRAWER RESCUE

The sock basket is where good intentions go to die. Here is the 20-minute rescue that actually sticks.

  1. Dump every sock in the house into one pile — every basket, every drawer, every stray from under the bed.
  2. Match pairs and evict the loners — if a sock has been single for two washes, it is done.
  3. Sort by type: baby, kids’, athletic, dress, colored crew, white crew, patterned and fun.
  4. Bag each type in a labeled ziplock — write the label big and bold so anyone can read it.
  5. Stand the bags upright in a basket or drawer, labels facing out — grab and go, no digging.

Luxe tip: A label means anyone in the house can put laundry away right — even the kids. That is the whole secret.

WINTER HEATING CHECKLIST

Winter is coming! Our friend Tyler at Cole's Appliance Repair shares his quick checklist to get your home ready BEFORE that first freezing night.

  1. Change your furnace filter — a dirty filter means restricted airflow and a harder-working system.
  2. Check your vents and registers — make sure they're open and keep furniture, rugs, and curtains clear.
  3. Clear the area around your furnace — keep boxes, cleaning supplies, and clutter away from heating equipment.
  4. Test your thermostat — turn your heat on BEFORE the first freezing night and make sure it's actually heating.
  5. Watch for warning signs — strange noises, unusual smells, weak airflow, or a home that won't stay warm.

Luxe tip: If something doesn't seem right, don't wait until you're wearing three blankets — call Tyler at Cole's Appliance Repair: 217-686-1735.

MOVE-IN / MOVE-OUT TIP

Moving in or moving out, the goal is the same — a home that feels like a fresh start. Here's how we do it.

  1. Top to bottom, back to front — dust ceiling fans and shelves first so you don't dirty what you already cleaned.
  2. Hit the spots landlords check: baseboards, inside cabinets, window tracks, and the tops of doors.
  3. Appliances earn the deposit — clean behind the fridge and run the oven's self-clean before you scrub.
  4. Finish with a daylight walk-through and your phone flashlight — stains love to hide in shadows.

Luxe tip: Pull the fridge out and unplug it the night before — clean behind it while it's cool, and give coils and floors time to dry. Never reach behind a hot fridge.

GARAGE CLEANUP TIP

Before the cold locks everything in place, give your garage one good day. This is the cleanup that makes the whole winter easier — and one Saturday morning gets it done.

  1. Pull everything out onto the driveway and sweep the floor clean — you can't organize what you can't see.
  2. Sort into three zones: keep it, toss it, donate it. Be honest — if you haven't touched it in two winters, it's clutter.
  3. Give the floor a real mop with a degreaser. Oil spots lift way easier than you'd think.
  4. Put back only the keepers on shelves and hooks — floor space is for the cars, not the boxes.
  5. Finish with a clear path to the snow shovel, salt, and the breaker box. Future you will be grateful.

Luxe tip: If you're tossing old paint cans, gas cans, or chemicals, don't pour anything down the drain — check your county's hazardous-waste drop-off instead. Your pipes (and your town's water) will thank you.

DEEP CLEAN TIP #17

Surface cleaning keeps a house livable — a deep clean makes it feel brand new again. Pick one room, take one good day, and work in this order. Trust me, the order is the whole trick.

  1. Start at the top: ceiling fan blades, vent covers, and cobweb corners. Dust falls DOWN, not up — so go high first and never clean the same spot twice.
  2. Move to the walls: wipe light switches, door handles, and baseboard edges. That's where the dust hides — and it's what your eye actually sees.
  3. Hit the blinds, windows, and mirrors next. Dry-dust the blinds FIRST, then glass cleaner. Clean glass makes the whole room feel brighter.
  4. Pull the furniture out once in a while. You'd be shocked what a year's worth of crumbs looks like behind the couch.
  5. Floors come LAST: vacuum every corner and under everything, then mop. Doing floors first just means doing them twice.

Luxe tip: Crack a window and run the vent fan when you're using anything strong — your lungs matter more than your baseboards. And never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia. That combo sends people to the ER.

ORGANIZING TIP #18

Coats, boots, backpacks, and everybody's keys — the entryway is where the whole house piles up first. Give it one honest hour and the rest of the house will feel calmer too.

  1. Empty it out first: pull everything off the hooks and out of the baskets. You can't organize a pile — you have to see what's actually living there.
  2. Keep only what gets used every day: one coat, one pair of shoes, one bag per person. The rest goes to its real home — closet, garage, or donation bag.
  3. Give every person one hook and one basket: coats up top, shoes below, keys in a little dish by the door. No spot means it lands on the floor.
  4. Finish with a five-minute nightly reset: shoes in the basket, bags on the hook, mail in one spot. Five minutes at night saves a half-hour hunt in the morning.

Luxe tip: Wet boots by the door? Put a boot tray down and wipe it out weekly — trapped water warps floors and grows mildew fast.

ORGANIZING TIP #19

Holiday cooking is coming, and a messy pantry makes every meal harder. Give it one honest hour now — you'll find what you already own before you buy it twice.

  1. Pull it all out and wipe the shelves down — crumbs hide everywhere.
  2. Toss what's expired, stale, or opened and forgotten. If you can't tell when you opened it, it's done.
  3. Group like with like: baking, cans, snacks, breakfast — one home for each.
  4. Clear bins up front, labels facing out, heavy stuff low so nothing topples.

Luxe tip: Check cans for dents, rust, or swelling — toss those, don't taste-test them. And keep cleaning sprays on a different shelf from food, never side by side.

CLEANING TIP #20

Company's coming for the holidays, and nobody's checking your closets — they're checking the bathroom, the kitchen, and where they sit. Focus there and the whole house feels clean.

  1. Start where guests go: entry, bathroom, kitchen, living room. The rest can wait.
  2. Clear the clutter first — a basket per room, toss it all in, sort it later.
  3. Wipe the spots people touch: light switches, door handles, faucet handles, the fridge door.
  4. Fresh towels, full soap, a lit candle — little touches that make it feel like you meant it.

Luxe tip: Never mix bleach with anything — not vinegar, not other cleaners. One cleaner at a time, and crack a window while you work.

WINE & CHAMPAGNE STAIN TIP

Spilled red wine or champagne? Don't panic — and don't rub it. Here's how to get it out before it sets.

  1. Blot right away with a clean cloth — press, don't rub, or you'll push it deeper into the fabric.
  2. For red wine, cover the spot with salt or baking soda to pull the wine out. Let it sit, then vacuum or brush it off.
  3. Rinse with cold water from the back of the fabric if you can — cold, never hot.
  4. For a stain that's still there, dab with a little dish soap and hydrogen peroxide on whites and light colors only, then wash as usual.
  5. Champagne is lighter — blot it up, rinse with cold water, and wash as usual. Don't let it dry first.

Luxe tip: The faster you get to it, the easier it comes out. And never use hot water on a wine stain — heat sets it for good.
